I tripled the price of my side project and lost zero users. Here's what I changed, in order.

For two years the app cost $3 a month. I picked that number because it felt safe. Nobody complained, which I took as a good sign. It wasn't. It meant the price was invisible.

The first thing I did was talk to the 12 people who had paid the longest. I asked one question: what would you use if this app disappeared tomorrow? Nine of them named a tool that cost more than $10.

Additionally, I looked at what they actually used. Almost nobody touched the features I was proudest of. Everyone used the weekly summary email. So I made that the headline of the pricing page.

Then I raised the price to $9 for new users only. Existing users kept $3 for life. That one decision removed every angry email before it was written.

Four weeks later, sign-ups were flat, not down. Revenue per new user was three times higher. Two old users asked to switch to the new price because they wanted to support the project.

The lesson I keep relearning: your price is a message. A low price says you are not sure it is worth it.
